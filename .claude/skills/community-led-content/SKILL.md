# Community-led content system

An adaptation of a creator-led growth operating system, with the conversion logic replaced. The creator model ends at subscription or purchase. This one continues up a participation ladder.

**The aim of every piece:** help a pro-European communicator overcome one real work friction, achieve a practical win, and move one step deeper into the community.

**The ladder:** discover → use → participate → contribute → advocate → host.

**What we own:** the gap between pro-European communicators' commitment and their access to practical craft, peers, and opportunities.

## Before writing anything

Fill in a one-line brief. Refuse to write without it.

- **One friction** (from the bank below)
- **One audience stage** (entrant, practitioner, organiser, leader/buyer, funder)
- **One claim**
- **One proof element**
- **One practical outcome** the reader can reach on Monday
- **One next step** on the ladder

If any slot is empty, the piece is not ready. Generic content that serves everyone at once serves no one.

## The friction bank

Every item addresses exactly one. Tag it by career stage so the piece stays specific.

| # | Friction | The reader's words |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Understanding | "I cannot translate the theory into an actual campaign decision." |
| 2 | Execution | "I know what to do, but not the sequence, tools, or first step." |
| 3 | Access | "The useful knowledge and people are behind institutional or financial barriers." |
| 4 | Trust | "This advice sounds plausible, but has it worked in a real European campaign?" |
| 5 | Isolation | "I am solving this alone and cannot compare my thinking with capable peers." |
| 6 | Career | "I need experience, recognition, contacts, or a credible route into the profession." |
| 7 | Organisational | "One-off training does not create lasting team capability." |
| 8 | Participation | "I might join, but I do not know anyone or what I could contribute." |
| 9 | Procurement | "I see the value, but cannot justify it internally." |
| 10 | Resource | "We have little money, time, or specialist capacity." |

Keep a running list of real questions, blockers and objections from sessions, replies and DMs. Content comes from that list, rather than from imagination.

## The five-beat structure

Replaces confront → simplify → prove → instruct → pay off.

1. **Recognise.** Name a situation the audience actually experiences. Recognise the tension without blaming the reader.
2. **Clarify.** Reduce it to one decision or one manageable model.
3. **Prove.** A real example, practitioner observation, tested process, or an honest limitation.
4. **Enable.** The steps, tool, template, or question sequence. Something usable on Monday.
5. **Connect.** The next relevant community action.

**Connect is the distinctive move.** The call to action is normally participation: compare approaches, bring a case to a clinic, use the template, come to a session, contribute an example, pass it to a colleague. Selling is the exception rather than the default.

## Segments

Write to one. Practitioners come first: they are the paying core, they produce the most credible cases, and they become contributors and champions.

- **Entrants.** Remove gatekeeping and theory overload. Offer portfolio artifacts, career pathways, mentoring, first professional connections.
- **Practitioners.** Time-saving workflows, live problems, campaign cases, leads, peer review, recognition.
- **Grassroots organisers.** Constrained time and budget. Accessible language. Equal standing rather than charity framing.
- **Leaders and buyers.** Team capability, retention, European connections, evidence, and why ongoing participation outlasts a workshop.
- **Funders.** Reach, activation, contribution, capability gains, and the independence protections around sponsored access.

## The seven reusable assets

1. **Friction bank** — real questions, blockers, objections, recurring work situations.
2. **Mission / solution / angle / format matrix** — topic selection by audience stage.
3. **Proof ledger** — approved cases, observations, metrics, quotes, screenshots, and limitations.
4. **Voice and judgment guide** — see below.
5. **Content brief** — the six slots above.
6. **Participation CTA library** — one action per rung of the ladder.
7. **Learning dashboard** — performance data plus qualitative evidence of what people used, questioned, or contributed.

## Production loop

Audience evidence → expert or member voice note → several hooks and formats → human selection → proof check → constitutional check → publish → engage → capture learning.

AI accelerates research, variants, repurposing and editing. AI does not invent the point of view, the evidence, or the political judgment.

## Mix

- 35% exact workflows and tools
- 20% cases and practitioner breakdowns
- 15% misconceptions and strategic reframes
- 15% member expertise and peer perspectives
- 10% career and access pathways
- 5% direct membership or event invitations

## Media formats

What a piece says and what it physically is are two separate decisions. Every week touches all seven, and a week with an empty column changes a piece's format rather than losing the slot.

| Media | What it is | Weekly floor |
|---|---|---|
| Video | Vertical, 30 to 60 seconds, captions burned in | 2 |
| Carousel | A slider, 5 to 9 cards, one idea per card | 2 |
| Photo | A real photograph of real people | 1 |
| Graphic | One designed card: a prompt, a statement, a date | 1 |
| Text | Words alone, the long-form anchor | 3 |
| Link | A post whose job is the click | 2 |
| Poll | A question that costs one tap to answer | 1 |

The poll is the cheapest contribution rung in the system: one tap is the smallest possible act of participation, and it tells you what the audience wants before you spend a day making it.

One asset becomes several media. The weekly anchor is written once, then cut: the newsletter becomes a text post, three of its points become a carousel, its sharpest line becomes a graphic, its example becomes a video.

A sustainable rhythm beats daily volume: three LinkedIn anchor posts a week, one deeper newsletter or public playbook contribution, one practitioner or member voice, a recurring Friday-style participation bridge, a monthly audience topic vote, and daily engagement without daily original publication.

## Testimonial Friday

Every Friday carries one testimonial, on the LinkedIn page. It is a standing slot, so the question each week is which proof goes out rather than whether any does.

Rules for the slot:

- Named person, named organisation, their own words, with permission on record. An anonymous quote is worth a fraction of a named one.
- Quote what they actually said. Tighten for length, never for effect, and never write the quote for them.
- Say what they have actually done with us. A new member describing why they joined is a true and useful testimonial; the same person described as a workshop graduate is a lie that one reply can expose.
- Pair the quote with the friction it answers, so the post teaches rather than boasts.
- End on participation: the session, the free workshop, the bootcamp.
- A Friday with no new permission runs an older quote again rather than inventing one.

## Voice

House rules, non-negotiable:

- The brand name is always lowercase: **european campaign playbook**.
- Never use em dashes in prose.
- Never use contrastive negation ("it is X, not Y"). State the positive claim.
- British English.
- Separate evidence, interpretation and recommendation.
- Never invent a stakeholder position, a quotation, or a legislative status.

## What not to import

- Aggressive accusations or blame aimed at the reader
- Unverified performance numbers
- Daily-volume obligation
- Superficial LinkedIn formatting tricks
- A funnel that treats people mainly as conversions

## Constitutional boundaries

These override engagement every time.

- Recognition instead of referral bounties
- Open access, with no visible second tier
- Sponsors may support access, and cannot control the editorial or learning agenda
- The community surfaces knowledge; the central team curates and enables it
- A strong hook must never collapse nuance, dignity, or cross-party credibility

## Success measures

Impressions are the weakest signal here. Measure:

- Percentage of content traceable to documented audience friction
- Resource use and qualified replies
- Newsletter-to-session and session-to-member movement
- Time to a new member's first useful outcome
- Contribution and repeat-participation rates
- Movement up the participation ladder
- Referral share of new members
- Member-to-organisation opportunities created
- Credible cases added to the proof ledger
